What is Modern Hebrew?

Modern Hebrew — called Ivrit (עברית) in Hebrew — is the everyday language of Israel. It is the language Israelis use to shop, argue, tell jokes, and watch the news. Around 10 million people speak Ivrit today as a first or second language.

Ivrit is quite different from Biblical Hebrew, which many people encounter in religious education. While Biblical Hebrew is an ancient literary language, Ivrit was revived as a living spoken language in the late 19th century — a globally unique phenomenon. Good to know

Hebrew is written from right to left. The alphabet has 22 letters — all consonants. Vowels are represented by dots and dashes below the letters (called Nikud), but in everyday writing they are usually omitted.

Is Hebrew difficult for German speakers?

The honest answer: Hebrew is different, but not as hard as many people think. There are challenges, but also surprising advantages.

What takes getting used to

New alphabet

22 letters that look nothing like the Latin alphabet. Most learners need 3–5 weeks to read fluently. At lingoStudio, you learn the alphabet systematically with exercises developed specifically for German-speaking learners.

Writing direction

Right to left. You get used to this faster than expected — usually by the second or third lesson.

New sounds

Some sounds don't exist in German or English, such as the guttural "Ch" (like the German "Bach", but deeper) or the "R" (rolled in the throat, similar to French). With targeted pronunciation training, these sounds become familiar quickly.

What works in your favour

Logical grammar: Hebrew has a root-based system (usually three consonants) from which words are formed following fixed patterns. Once you understand the principle, you can often figure out new words on your own.

Simple sentence structure: The basic structure is Subject – Verb – Object. In the present tense, there is no verb "to be" — "I am tired" literally translates to "I tired" (אני עייף). This makes first sentences surprisingly easy.

No grammatical cases: Unlike German, Hebrew has no Dativ, Akkusativ, or Genitiv. A significant advantage if you already struggle with German grammar.

What you learn in a beginner course (level A1)

In the A1 course at lingoStudio in Zurich, you progress through these stages over one semester (20 lessons × 60 minutes):

1–4

The alphabet and first words

You learn the letters, basic vowels, and start reading and writing simple words. You can introduce yourself: name, origin, profession.

5–10

First conversations

Greetings, numbers, colours, family, ordering food, asking for directions. You form short sentences and understand simple questions.

11–16

Grammar foundations

Present tense, gender (masculine/feminine), singular/plural, negation, question words. You hold simple conversations about everyday life.

17–20

Review and practice

Revision, free conversation, reading short texts, understanding songs. By the end of A1, you can communicate in simple everyday situations in Hebrew.

How long does it take to learn Hebrew?

This depends on your goal and how much you practise. Here is a realistic estimate:

Level A1 — Basics (3–6 months)

You can introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, read short texts, and manage basic everyday situations. At lingoStudio, you reach this level in one semester (20 lessons) with regular practice at home.

Level A2 — Elementary (6–12 months)

You understand frequently used expressions, can talk about familiar topics, and read simple texts. You begin to understand newspaper articles and songs.

Level B1 — Intermediate (1–2 years)

You can handle most everyday situations, describe experiences, and express your opinion. You understand TV programmes and can read newspapers.
